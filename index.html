Executive Summary
Strategic briefing — 6 May 2026

FY2026 performance: Revenue is tracking at 446B VND against a 303B contract target — a +47.2% outperformance. Year-to-date invoiced: 360B with the fiscal year still closing. Interactive sliders: Two sliders control the forecast. The Volume slider adjusts growth assumptions (0% = pure trend). The Price slider adjusts pricing level. They multiply together: +10% volume and +5% price ≈ +15.5% total uplift. Charts and cards update in real-time.

P8
Volume Dashboard
Converts revenue into physical units for operations — how many units to make, ship, and store
↓

What it answers: “How many physical units do we need?” Revenue forecasts must translate into product quantities for production, logistics, and warehouse planning.

The Bridge formula (plain language): Units = Revenue ÷ (Price Factor × Selling Price per Unit). The Price Factor accounts for each customer’s specific discount rate and VAT status. A customer with a 20% discount needs more units to generate the same revenue as a customer with a 5% discount. The bridge uses customer-level pricing for the vast majority of pairs — validated against historical data with near-zero error.

Slider interaction: Moving the volume slider up increases both revenue and units. Moving the price slider up increases revenue but decreases units (higher price = fewer units needed). This distinction matters for operations.

What to look for: UHT is the volume leader but a smaller revenue contributor (high-volume, low-price). PL is the opposite — lower volume but highest revenue. Volume and value do not move in lockstep.

Unit convention

All volumes are in primary selling units: Hộp (tin/box) or Túi (bag) — not Thùng (cases). The PO Recommendation column translates these into actionable order quantities.

What it answers: “Which customers buy which products, and where are the gaps?” This page turns the customer portfolio into a sales action map.

The penetration matrix is the core view. Rows = customers, columns = SKU families. Filled cells = active buying relationship. What to look for: A fully-filled row means a high-penetration account buying across the range. A row with only 1–2 products is a cross-sell opportunity. A flagged (dormant) cell is a “warm lead” — the customer already knows the product and has buying infrastructure in place.

Revenue concentration: If the top 10% of customers drive the vast majority of revenue, the business is concentrated. Penetration improvement in the mid-tier has the most growth leverage.

Key concepts behind the model

Weighted Run Rate (WRR)
The company’s “monthly earning power.” Blends the last 3 months (50%), 6 months (30%), and 12 months (20%) into a single rate. Currently 46.8B VND/month pre-retention, 39.2B post-retention. A rising WRR means the business is accelerating.
Five-Layer Forecast
Each layer adds one adjustment: (1) Run Rate → (2) Seasonality (Tết surge/dip) → (3) Retention (churn risk) → (4) Scenario sliders → (5) Governance guardrails. Output = monthly projection per customer × SKU.
Bridge (Value → Volume)
Converts revenue into physical units: Volume = Sale Value ÷ (Price Factor × ASP). Uses each customer’s actual pricing terms where available, falling back to portfolio averages for new customers.
Retention Rate
Probability a customer will still be buying next year. New customers get a lower rate (less certainty). Long-standing customers are assumed to continue. Currently 83.8% overall — meaning 16.2% of earning power is at risk of churn.
Seasonality Index
Monthly adjustment for natural demand patterns. Pre-Tết months surge; post-Tết dips. The index ensures the forecast follows the actual demand calendar rather than assuming every month is equal.
Stock Coverage
Months of inventory remaining at the current sell-through rate. (SOH + In-Transit) ÷ monthly demand. Below 1 month = critical. 1–2.5 = monitor. Above 2.5 = safe. Target buffer: 2.5 months.
